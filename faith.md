# Faith = Word of God × Trust

Faith is often described as believing in what we cannot see. But in the Christian life, faith is more than believing that God exists. It means depending on who He is and what He has said. The expression “Faith = the Word of God × Trust” shows this connection: God’s Word is the foundation, and trust is our response.

God’s Word gives faith its direction. Without a firm foundation, belief can become wishful thinking. Scripture shows us God’s character, His promises, and His plans. Romans 10:17 teaches that faith comes through hearing the message of Christ. This message points us not just to something to believe, but to Someone we can trust.

Yet knowing God’s Word is not the same as trusting it. We may memorize verses about God’s care and still find it hard to depend on Him. Trust puts what we know into practice. It means relying on God when life is uncertain and choosing to obey Him even when we do not know what will happen.

The multiplication sign shows how God’s Word and our trust work together. Knowing that a bridge is strong is different from walking across it. In the same way, knowing God’s promises is different from relying on them in daily life. Our trust does not make God’s Word true; it shapes how we respond to its truth.

This does not mean that real faith never struggles. Believers still face fear, grief, and unanswered questions. The prayer “I believe; help my unbelief!” in Mark 9:24 shows that we can trust God while being honest about our weakness. Faith grows not by pretending we have no doubts, but by bringing those doubts to Him.

This equation is a picture, not a way to measure someone’s faith. Faith cannot be given a score, and stronger trust does not promise an easier life. Instead, the expression reminds us that Christian faith rests on what God has made known and is lived out by depending on Him.

God’s Word tells us whom we can trust. Trust puts that knowledge into practice. Together, they describe a faith that listens, relies, and acts.
