# Practice Is How We Grow

We often think of practice as something we do to become very good at something. We practice to win a race, play an instrument, or pass a test. But practice is not just about success. It is part of how we grow. Before we can aim to do something well, we must learn how to move, communicate, and live in the world.

Even before birth, something like practice begins. In the womb, a baby moves its arms and legs, swallows the fluid around it, and makes movements like breathing, though it still gets oxygen through the placenta. These actions are not planned, but they help prepare the body for life outside the womb. Long before a child knows what effort means, its body is already at work.

After birth, this process is easier to see. A baby reaches for a toy and misses, then tries again. It babbles before it speaks and tries many times before it can stand. What an adult might call failure is simply part of learning. Not every attempt has to succeed to be useful.

As we get older, we often lose patience with this process. We want to feel sure of ourselves before we begin and become skilled before anyone sees us try. We compare our early efforts with someone else’s best work, forgetting how much practice came before it. We may think that struggling means we lack ability, when it may just mean we are still learning.

Practice does not make us perfect. Repeating the same mistake over and over does not always help us improve. Good practice means paying attention, getting feedback, resting, and being willing to try a different way. Even so, we usually need repeated effort to build our skills. Knowing what to do is not the same as being able to do it.

The lesson is simple: we do not need to feel ashamed of needing practice. Trying again is not a sign that we are not good enough. It is a normal part of growth.

If preparation begins even before birth, learning through repeated attempts is not a weakness we need to outgrow. It is how we build our abilities over time. We begin before we are ready, and through effort, guidance, and time, we become more able.
