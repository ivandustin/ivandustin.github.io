# Love Is Not a Feeling

The love Jesus calls us to practice is not a feeling. It is a choice to seek another person’s good, lived out through kindness, giving, forgiveness, and service. Feelings can move us to act, but they change. Love does not depend on whether we feel warm, moved, or close to someone. It calls us to do good even when it is hard or costs us something.

Jesus shows this in the story of the Good Samaritan. A wounded man lies beside the road. Two religious leaders pass him by, but a Samaritan stops to help. He treats the man’s wounds, takes him to a safe place, and pays for his care. He does not just feel sorry for the man; he meets his needs. Jesus ends the story with a command: “Go and do likewise” (Luke 10:37).

This does not mean feelings have no value. Jesus himself showed care, sadness, and affection. Such feelings can accompany love, but they are not love itself. Feeling sorry for a wounded person does not help him if we leave him beside the road. Love puts care into action by feeding the hungry, welcoming the lonely, and helping those in need, whether or not we feel like doing so.

Jesus also told his followers to love their enemies and pray for those who mistreated them (Matthew 5:44). He calls us to choose their good; he does not ask us to manufacture a feeling of affection. We cannot always make ourselves feel fond of someone who has hurt us. Yet we can choose not to take revenge, refuse to be cruel, and seek what is good and fair. This does not mean accepting harm or staying in danger. It means refusing to let hatred guide our actions.

Jesus showed this love when he washed his disciples’ feet. He took the place of a servant and did a simple, humble task for them. Then he told them to follow his example (John 13:14–15). Love was not just something to talk about. It meant being willing to serve and meet a real need.

This giving love is shown most fully in the cross. Jesus said that the greatest love is to lay down one’s life for one’s friends (John 15:13). His words show that love means giving of ourselves for another person’s good. Love is measured by that willingness to give, not by the strength of a feeling.

In daily life, love takes simple, practical forms. It listens when someone needs to speak. It brings food, keeps promises, tells the truth with kindness, and helps without seeking praise. Its purpose is to care for people in ways that truly help. We do not have to wait for a loving feeling before doing a loving thing. Love is a choice we keep putting into practice, even as our feelings change.
